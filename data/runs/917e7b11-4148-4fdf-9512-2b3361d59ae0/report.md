## What the field agrees on

IoT devices for women's safety commonly integrate GPS and GSM technologies to provide continuous location tracking and real-time alerts [doi:10.2174/9798898810450125010012, doi:10.62643/ijerst.2025.v21.i2.pp712-722, doi:10.48175/ijarsct-24973]. These systems are designed to dispatch distress signals, including location data, to registered contacts, relatives, and emergency services like police stations [doi:10.2174/9798898810450125010012, doi:10.1109/icses66558.2026.11479101, doi:10.1109/netcrypt65877.2025.11102618]. The integration of IoT with real-time communication is seen as a robust, proactive method to enhance women's safety and prevent harm in public spaces [doi:10.47392/irjaem.2025.0288]. Some systems are also being developed to connect directly with police or emergency dispatch centers for intervention [doi:10.48175/ijarsct-24973].

## Where the field disagrees

A contradiction exists regarding the primary microcontroller used in IoT-based women's safety systems. One paper states that the system uses a NodeMCU microcontroller as its main processor, connecting to multiple sensors and modules [doi:10.1109/icpcsn65854.2025.11034876]. In contrast, another paper describes a system that combines GPS, GSM, Raspberry Pi Pico, and ESP32-CAM, among other components, to deliver communication and self-defense features [doi:10.1109/icoecit68303.2026.11497989]. This disagreement regarding the core processing unit remains unresolved within the provided corpus.
