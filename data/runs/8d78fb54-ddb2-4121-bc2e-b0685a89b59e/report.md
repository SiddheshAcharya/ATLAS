## What the field agrees on

The field agrees that IoT devices for women's safety aim to provide rapid emergency response support using advanced technology [doi:10.1109/netcrypt65877.2025.11102618, doi:10.1109/incip64058.2025.11020103]. These systems are designed to continuously monitor the user's surroundings and automatically dispatch distress signals with location data to contacts and emergency services for various emergencies, including assaults, medical incidents, and falls [doi:10.1109/netcrypt65877.2025.11102618]. The devices commonly incorporate technologies such as Arduino, Raspberry Pi, GPS, and GSM to send notifications [doi:10.55041/isjem.acme148, doi:10.62643/ijerst.2025.v21.i2.pp712-722]. Additionally, components like accelerometers and microphones are included to enable functionalities such as location tracking, fall detection, and sound analysis [doi:10.1109/netcrypt65877.2025.11102618]. The development of these IoT solutions is motivated by the claim that existing safety measures, including wearable and smartphone systems, are currently insufficient and face problems like network connectivity and immediate help [doi:10.1109/icears64219.2025.10941429].
